The three kinds of coverage we'll compare.

Most people choose between Medicare Advantage and Original Medicare with a Supplement. Part D covers prescriptions.

Medicare Advantage

Plans from private insurance companies that replace Original Medicare for your Part A and Part B coverage. Most include drug coverage and use a network of doctors. Some include extra benefits, such as dental or vision.

Medicare Supplement

Also called Medigap. These plans work alongside Original Medicare and help pay costs like deductibles and coinsurance. You can see any doctor nationwide who accepts Medicare.

Part D drug plans

Stand-alone prescription coverage for people with Original Medicare. Each plan has its own list of covered drugs and pharmacies, so we check yours.

Questions people ask first.

Does it cost anything to work with you?

No. There is no charge for my help. Like most Medicare brokers, I'm paid by the insurance company if you enroll in a plan. A plan's premium is the same whether you enroll through me, through the insurance company, or through Medicare.gov.

Are you part of Medicare or the government?

No. I'm a licensed, independent insurance agent. I'm not connected with Medicare, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), or Social Security. For official information, visit Medicare.gov or call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227; TTY 1-877-486-2048), 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

Can I keep my doctors?

It depends on the coverage. With Original Medicare and a Supplement, you can see any provider in the country who accepts Medicare. Most Medicare Advantage plans use networks, so I check your doctors before you choose.

What happens when we meet?

Before we discuss specific Medicare Advantage or Part D plans, Medicare rules require us to agree on and record the topics we'll cover. This is called a Scope of Appointment. It's done in writing for in-person meetings or recorded on a phone call, and it can be done the same day. You're never obligated to enroll, and you can end the conversation at any time.
